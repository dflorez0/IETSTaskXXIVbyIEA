Integrated Composite Curves
Unit
How to read this plot

Definition. The grand composite curve gives the net heat requirement of the integration as a whole, without attributing it to individual units. The integrated composite curve partitions the system into the selected unit and its complement. Separated (dashed) is the heat profile of the selected unit; base (solid) is the profile of the remaining units. The region enclosed between the two curves represents the heat exchanged between them at each temperature level.

Interpretation.

  • The ordinate is the shifted temperature, corrected by each stream's ΔTmin/2 contribution. Vertical overlap therefore indicates thermodynamically feasible exchange. The abscissa is the heat load.
  • The horizontal distance between the curves at a given temperature is the heat transferred between the selected unit and the remainder of the system at that level. Where the distance vanishes, no exchange takes place.
  • A net heat source extends towards negative heat load; a net heat sink extends towards positive heat load.
  • Points at which the curves meet are pinch points. No heat is transferred across them.
  • QHU and QCU denote the residual hot and cold utility requirements, which cannot be satisfied internally.

Application.

  • Proximity of the separated curve to the base curve indicates appropriate placement. A large horizontal separation indicates heat supplied well above, or removed well below, the temperature required, and the corresponding levels should be revised.
  • A temperature range over which the curves coincide indicates that no exchange with the remainder of the system is available; the requirement must be met by a utility.
  • The scaling factor f in the left panel scales the profile of the selected unit proportionally, and may be used to size a utility against a given pocket of the base curve.
  • The Process and Utilities options aggregate the respective groups, allowing the two halves of the system to be compared directly.
  • Whole system takes every model as the subject, leaving no complement. The base then collapses to a vertical line at QHU and the band between the curves is the system grand composite curve itself, which is the view to use for the overall heat recovery pockets rather than for the contribution of any one unit.
